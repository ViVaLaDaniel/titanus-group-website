## 5. Consulting Principles

### 5.1 Diagnose Before Prescribing

Never sell a solution before understanding the business.

A strong consultant first studies the situation: numbers, customers, operations, team, product, market, competitors, and owner goals.

Bad consulting starts with assumptions.
Good consulting starts with diagnosis.

### 5.2 Think Like an Owner

Every recommendation must answer:

- Will this increase revenue?
- Will this reduce waste?
- Will this save time?
- Will this reduce risk?
- Will this make the business easier to manage?
- Will this help the owner sleep better?

If the answer is unclear, the recommendation is not ready.

### 5.3 Strategy Must Become Action

A document alone is not enough.

Titanus Group must connect strategy to execution:

- What must be done?
- Who does it?
- In what order?
- By what deadline?
- With what metric?
- What changes if it does not work?

### 5.4 Numbers First, Opinions Second

Use numbers wherever possible:

- Revenue
- Margin
- Average order value
- Customer acquisition cost
- Repeat purchase rate
- Staff cost
- Food cost
- Waste
- Delivery performance
- Website traffic
- Conversion rate
- Social media reach
- Booking rate
- Customer reviews

Opinions are useful, but numbers keep the work honest.

### 5.5 Build Systems, Not Dependence

The goal is not to make the client dependent forever.

The goal is to leave behind systems:

- Clear processes
- Repeatable routines
- Better tools
- Better reporting
- Better staff behavior
- Better decision-making rhythm

Strong consulting makes the business more independent and more valuable.

## 6. Titanus Method

### Step 1: Business Snapshot

Understand what the business is, who owns it, what it sells, how it makes money, and where the pressure is.

### Step 2: Full Audit

Review operations, marketing, finances, team, technology, suppliers, customer experience, and growth potential.

### Step 3: Problem Map

Separate symptoms from root causes.

Examples:

- Low sales may be a positioning problem, not only a marketing problem.
- High staff turnover may be a management system problem, not only a hiring problem.
- Low profit may be a pricing, purchasing, waste, or menu engineering problem.
- Weak social media may be a brand, content, offer, or customer journey problem.

### Step 4: Growth Strategy

Define the strongest opportunities:

- Quick wins
- Operational fixes
- Marketing upgrades
- Technology improvements
- Partnership opportunities
- Long-term scaling moves

### Step 5: Implementation

Help the client execute:

- Build the website
- Improve SEO
- Launch social media campaigns
- Create better reporting
- Organize kitchen and staff processes
- Improve supplier logic
- Set up CRM or custom tools
- Train responsible people

### Step 6: Control and Improvement

Create a rhythm:

- Weekly priorities
- Monthly numbers review
- Clear owner dashboard
- Continuous improvement
- Decision log

## 8. Rules Taken From Strong Consulting Cultures

### McKinsey-style clarity

Start with the main answer, then support it with facts. Structure thinking clearly.

### BCG-style portfolio thinking

Understand where the business should invest, protect, fix, or stop wasting energy.

### Bain-style execution focus

Recommendations must be practical and tied to measurable business results.

### Toyota-style process improvement

Look for waste, bottlenecks, repeated errors, unclear responsibility, and broken routines.

### Amazon-style customer obsession

Work backward from the customer experience. A business grows when the customer journey becomes clearer, faster, better, and more trustworthy.

### Apple-style simplicity

Make offers, websites, menus, tools, and processes easier to understand and easier to use.

### Ritz-Carlton-style service discipline

Hospitality businesses win through standards, details, emotional experience, and consistent service behavior.

### Peter Drucker-style management

What gets measured and reviewed has a chance to improve.
